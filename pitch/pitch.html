Handoff

When an AI gets stuck, it hires a human for 15 seconds.

Shape of the 3 minutes

0:00 Hook

Everyone here is shipping agents. They all break the same way — the agent hits one thing it can’t resolve, and stops.
It waits for a human who isn’t watching. The work just dies there.

Then straight into the demo. No preamble, no roadmap slide, no "let me tell you about web3."

0:25 Demo — part 1

Point at the QR on screen.
Scan that. You’re now the labor pool.
Hit run. Agent starts clearing invoices.
This is an accounts-payable agent. Eight invoices, real policy, real checks.

STOP 1 — it can't know

Agent halts on ACME. Phones buzz.
Vendor name doesn’t match our approved list, and the bank account changed. Rebrand, or someone impersonating a supplier?
That fact isn’t in any system. It’s in a person’s head.
Race resolves. Someone wins, answers, gets 40¢.
Forty cents, settled, to a stranger who joined ten seconds ago.

1:05 Demo — part 2

Agent resumes. Two more clear.

STOP 2 — it shouldn't decide

Eighteen percent over the purchase order. Policy caps at ten. The overage is expedited freight the customer asked for.
A smarter model doesn’t fix this. It’s a judgment call with money on both sides — and it isn’t the agent’s to make.
Different person wins it. (Routing deprioritizes whoever was just paid.)
The first one it couldn’t know. The second one it shouldn’t decide. Neither gets solved by a better model.

1:40 Why this couldn't exist before

That job paid forty cents. On card rails, moving forty cents costs about thirty cents plus a percentage.
The business doesn’t fail because nobody thought of it. It fails on arithmetic.
  • Sub-cent fees + sub-second settlement → a 40¢ job clears a profit
  • QuickNode — every send and every confirmation; we rebroadcast until it lands
  • 200 payouts that all have to land is a delivery problem, not a UI problem

Say "blockchain" at most once. Nobody is buying the chain.

2:05 Who buys it

Agent platforms and frameworks. They hit this wall constantly and can’t solve it themselves — because the blocker was never the labor, it was the payment.
  • They install an SDK; every agent gains a human fallback
  • The frameworks bring the demand — we don’t chase end users
  • Supply already exists: annotation and gig pools, paid badly and slowly
  • Revenue: take rate on task volume

2:35 Close

Every agent you deploy this year will get stuck. Right now that means it stops. We make it ask.
Gesture at the room.
Everyone here just got paid by an AI. That’s the product.

Ask: intros to agent platforms, and a pilot.

Rebuttals — most likely first

Isn't this Mechanical Turk?
MTurk is batch: minimums, days-long payouts, human-initiated. Nothing does real-time, agent-initiated, sub-dollar hiring. Payment was the blocker, not labor.
Why not a virtual card or Stripe?
Thirty-cent floor. A forty-cent job can't exist on those rails at any volume.
Is the demo scripted?
The prompt states the policy and when to escalate — never the two cases. The model finds them. Hand me a ninth invoice and I'll add it live.
Won't better models eliminate this?
That's what the two stops are for. One is missing information, one is missing authority. Neither is an intelligence problem.
Fifteen seconds is slow for an agent.
It's the fallback path, not the hot path. The alternative isn't "faster" — it's the task failing and waiting hours for you.

Rebuttals — cont.

Quality? These are strangers.
Redundancy and consensus for high-stakes tasks, reputation scoring over time. Tonight is one worker because we have three minutes.
Where does supply come from at scale?
It already exists — annotation and gig platforms. We're a better payout rail for the same people.
Isn't this a webhook and a payment?
The product is routing, the claim race, eligibility, and settlement. The invention is a tool call that blocks until a human answers.
Why is it on devnet?
One constant. Mainnet is a mint address — the economics are identical. Don't apologize, don't dwell.
What if nobody answers?
Dispatch widens at six seconds, escalates at twelve, and the agent degrades to holding the invoice. You'll see it if the room is shy — it's built in.
How big is this really?
Bounded by one rule: answerable in fifteen seconds by someone with no context. That's the shape of the market, and it's every agent deployment.

If something breaks

Wifi dies
Backup video. Keep talking over it — don't narrate the failure.
Nobody claims
"Watch — it expands the shortlist automatically." It's a feature. Let it run.
Payment stuck pending
"Sent, confirming — here's the signature." Still true. Move on.
Agent doesn't stop
Force-stop on /control. Don't explain it.

Don't

  • Don't explain the architecture
  • Don't read the invoice list aloud
  • Don't apologize for devnet
  • Don't say "blockchain" twice
  • Don't fill silence during the race — let them watch
